I'm originally from Perth: a beautiful, peaceful (some might say too peaceful!) little city on Australia's west coast.

Computers were always around. My parents were Apple fans from the 80s, so there was always a Mac in the house. I played a lot of games on it (Return to Zork, anyone?), and it was tinkering with mods for those games that first pulled me into graphics and animation.

That interest turned into a Bachelor of Science in Digital Media, a course that sat halfway between computer science and design. One week it was colour theory, the next it was graphics code for games. It suited me perfectly, and after graduating in 2007 I carried it straight into my first job as a frontend web developer: living between the designers and the engineers, turning static designs into working sites.

Then the iPhone arrived in Australia. A computer in my pocket, always online, with graphics that felt like the web on steroids. I bought an app development book, went to every iOS meetup I could find, and started shipping my own apps to the App Store. By 2013 the hobby had outgrown the day job, and I switched careers to iOS engineering for good.

I've been at it full-time for just over a decade now, and I've had the time of my life. Those side projects opened doors to great companies in Japan and the US, and took me around the world to conferences, meeting even more amazing people along the way.

These days I'm at a brand-new startup in Tokyo, still shipping my own apps and open-source libraries on the side. Technology is moving faster than at any point in my career, and I can't wait to see what it lets me build next.

Nice to meet you!

Why Japan?

A thread that started with six months abroad at age nine, and never really ended.

My family's ties to Japan go back a long way. When I was nine, Dad was offered a six-month secondment here and brought the whole family along. My sister and I were dropped into a local elementary school where barely anyone spoke English. I had no idea what was going on. I just knew it was an adventure.

And it was. We travelled somewhere new almost every weekend and made friends we're still close with today. Back in Australia, Japan never really left: I studied Japanese through high school and university, represented the WA government on a scholarship tour of its sister cities in Hyogo, and later volunteered at Japanese cultural events around Perth and ran conversation nights to help newcomers make friends.

So when my career meant leaving Perth (WA runs on mining, not tech), the shortlist was Melbourne, the US, or Japan. Melbourne felt too close. The US felt too far. Japan was just right.

It still is. Living here puts me in the middle of one of the world's great tech hubs, yet close enough to Australia that home never feels far away. That it's also one of the most beautiful and safest countries on Earth is no accident. Japan works hard at both, and I don't take living in the result for granted.

From here, my goal is to keep helping bridge Japan's iOS community with the rest of the world, in both languages.

About This Site

[tagline placeholder] Built with Ignite, designed with Claude, served as flat files.

For most of its life, tim.dev ran on WordPress — a setup that served me well for the better part of a decade, but had quietly grown into more maintenance than writing. This version is a clean rebuild: a fully static site generated by Ignite, the Swift-based generator by Paul Hudson.

The visual language — the type, the palette, the rhythm of the pages — is mine, decided the old-fashioned way over a lot of evenings. The build itself owes an honest amount to Claude Design and Claude Code, which I leaned on to translate the design into a working site. A genuine collaboration, and a fast one.

No database, no admin panel, no plugins to keep up to date — just files, hosted on GitHub Pages, served straight from the repo.
