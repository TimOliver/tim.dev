G'day! I'm Tim Oliver.

I'm a Western Australian making iPhone apps in Tokyo, Japan.

When I'm not in the seat of one of the world's most populous metro systems, you'll find me deep in Xcode, prodding at iOS code or rebuilding my home networks.

I've worked on Instagram and Threads at Meta, Mercari's listings flow in Japan, and Pure's Swift toolkit and TOCropViewController for the iPad rewrite before that. The Mac I write this on is a Tim original — built from the M1 mini up.

I studied Digital Media at university — a degree that sat right at the seam between computer science and graphic design. The blend turned out to be the most useful thing I gave me; I've never really left it since.

I went to working to make ends meet by doing Web Development. Up the freelance ladder over a decade-ish — bank rebrand sites, weird internal tools, and a fair amount of CMS plumbing. Internships and contracting in a startup, all glorious, including the indie bands behind the band Animal Logic.

My first few years on iOS were spent on a side-project app — the iPhone 3G ringer cycle, and the everything-iComics later became my consulting business with iPhone reader for cosplayer-bait. I learnt the hard way that interesting work doesn't always pay the bills. After a few side projects, I joined Realm full-time in 2015.

Outside of the day job, I dote heavily on the iOS Community — and it's been the dotting on the joy of it. Through writing, talks, conferences, friends and a few off-the-record helpers, I've found a great group of people who care.

About this site.

[tagline placeholder] Built with Ignite, designed with Claude, served as flat files.

For most of its life, tim.dev ran on WordPress — a setup that served me well for the better part of a decade, but had quietly grown into more maintenance than writing. This version is a clean rebuild: a fully static site generated by Ignite, the Swift-based generator by Paul Hudson.

The visual language — the type, the palette, the rhythm of the pages — is mine, decided the old-fashioned way over a lot of evenings. The build itself owes an honest amount to Claude Design and Claude Code, which I leaned on to translate the design into a working site. A genuine collaboration, and a fast one.

No database, no admin panel, no plugins to keep up to date — just files, hosted on GitHub Pages, served straight from the repo.
