I'm originally from Perth: a beautiful, peaceful (some might say too peaceful!) little city on Australia's west coast.

My parents were Apple fans from the 80s, so there was always a Mac in the house, and tinkering with game mods on it (Return to Zork, anyone?) is what first pulled me into graphics and animation. That became a Digital Media degree, half computer science and half design, and then a first job as a frontend web developer, turning the designers’ mockups into working HTML, CSS, JavaScript and Flash (if anyone remembers Flash!).

Then the iPhone arrived in Australia. A computer in my pocket, always online. I bought an app development book, went to every iOS meetup I could find, and started shipping my own apps to the App Store. By 2013 the hobby had outgrown the day job, and I switched to iOS engineering for good.

Those side projects opened doors to great companies in Japan and the US, and in 2018 work brought me to Tokyo, which I've called home ever since. Between the apps I've worked on and the open-source libraries I've published, I've been privileged to ship code to hundreds of millions of Apple devices. It's been the time of my life.

These days I'm at a brand-new startup in Tokyo, still shipping my own apps and libraries on the side. Technology is moving faster than at any point in my career, and I can't wait to see what it lets me build next.

Nice to meet you!

About This Site

[tagline placeholder] Built with Ignite, designed with Claude, served as flat files.

For most of its life, tim.dev ran on WordPress — a setup that served me well for the better part of a decade, but had quietly grown into more maintenance than writing. This version is a clean rebuild: a fully static site generated by Ignite, the Swift-based generator by Paul Hudson.

The visual language — the type, the palette, the rhythm of the pages — is mine, decided the old-fashioned way over a lot of evenings. The build itself owes an honest amount to Claude Design and Claude Code, which I leaned on to translate the design into a working site. A genuine collaboration, and a fast one.

No database, no admin panel, no plugins to keep up to date — just files, hosted on GitHub Pages, served straight from the repo.
