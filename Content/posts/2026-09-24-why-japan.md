My family's ties to Japan go back a long way. When I was nine, Dad was offered a six-month secondment here and brought the whole family along. My sister and I were dropped into a local elementary school where barely anyone spoke English. I had no idea what was going on. I just knew it was an adventure.

And it was. We travelled somewhere new almost every weekend and made friends we're still close with today. Back in Australia, Japan never really left: I studied Japanese through high school and university, represented the WA government on a scholarship tour of its sister cities in Hyogo, and later volunteered at Japanese cultural events around Perth and ran conversation nights to help newcomers make friends.

So when my career meant leaving Perth (WA runs on mining, not tech), the shortlist was Melbourne, the US, or Japan. Melbourne felt too close. The US felt too far. Japan was just right.

It still is. Living here puts me in the middle of one of the world's great tech hubs, yet close enough to Australia that home never feels far away. That it's also one of the most beautiful and safest countries on Earth is no accident. Japan works hard at both, and I don't take living in the result for granted.

From here, my goal is to keep helping bridge Japan's iOS community with the rest of the world, in both languages.
