iComics has been my pride and joy for well over a decade now. Between the talks I've given about it and the libraries I've open-sourced from it, it's responsible for a lot of the iOS jobs I've had over the years.

When I bought my first iPad, I immediately saw how amazing it could be for reading comics: far better than a PC monitor, and even the (at the time, non-Retina) iPhone.

The big comic book companies all had their own reader apps for their own comics, which made me realize there was an untapped gap: an open comic reader for everything else.

These days, the internet is full of DRM-free comics, many of them sold through places like Humble Bundle, DriveThruComics and even Kickstarter. The goal of iComics is to be the catch-all reader for any comic that doesn't have its own dedicated app.

That being said, it's been *incredibly* challenging. When users bring their own files, they come in all sorts of unusual shapes and sizes, so building a reader that's both fast and memory-efficient has been a very fun journey.
